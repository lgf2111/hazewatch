About the data

What you are looking at. NASA publishes the fires it sees from space, free, every day. Two cameras, five satellites: MODIS flies on Terra and Aqua, VIIRS on S-NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21. We put both onto one grid: a square counts as burning on a day if either camera saw fire in it.

What is missing. The live view is one week. One month, September 2015, comes from the NASA archive. Neither supports a claim about a season or a year-on-year change. One of NASA's four files came back empty the day we fetched it, VIIRS S-NPP. It is shown as empty, not filled in.

Why is the VIIRS S-NPP file empty? It is not a glitch: the Suomi NPP satellite stopped delivering new data at 13:00 UTC on 2 November 2026 (NASA Earthdata alert).

What this is not. Not a haze forecast, not PSI. The wind is the surface wind at one point per province, and it does not carry the smoke on its own.

One scale for both cameras

VIIRS grades each detection low, nominal or high. MODIS gives a number from 0 to 100, so we cut it into the same three grades. Low detections are left out of the map and the calendar.

One set of columns for both cameras

What counting squares changes

Counting squares narrows the gap between the cameras but does not close it: VIIRS sees smaller fires than MODIS can, and the satellites pass at different times.

Every number is in data/facts.json; the raw files (data/raw) and the code (tools/build_snapshot.py) are on GitHub.
